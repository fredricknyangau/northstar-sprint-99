# Day 2 Reflection

## What Happened

- I continued working in my QA and Documentation role for the Northstar Sprint Support Detection MVP. 
- I focused on understanding how the support-deflection flows can be implemented and how they aligned with the requirements established in the Charter and Board during our first meeting. I also started thinking and researched on the different scenarios that would need to be tested by the QA engineer, including successful interactions, invalid inputs, and error states.

## What Went Well ##

- I was able to gain a better understanding of how the MVP should work from the user's perspective. 
- Again reviewing the expected support flows helped me to identify what should be checked during the manual QA process. 
- I also became more familiar with the team's GitHub workflow and the importance of keeping documentation and project evidence aligned with the actual work being completed.

## What Was Harder Than Expected ##

One challenge was translating the project requirements into practical QA checks. It required me to think beyond whether a feature simply works and consider what happens when users provide unexpected input or when something goes wrong. Keeping track of the documentation while the build was progressing was also something I needed to manage carefully.

## Self-Assessment ##

- I made progress in understanding the QA requirements and preparing for testing, but I still need to become more confident in systematically testing each user flow and documenting issues with enough detail for the development team to act on them.
